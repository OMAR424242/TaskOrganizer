Privacy

One Thing · last updated 23 September 2026

One Thing works with no account, and in that state nothing you type ever leaves your device. If you choose to make an account, your tasks sync between your own devices and nowhere else.

That is the whole of it, but the rest of this page says it precisely, because "we value your privacy" is not an answer and both app stores ask for a real one.

Without an account

The app has no account until you make one, and it never asks twice. Until then:

  • Your tasks, topics, history, settings and the picture you may have set as a wallpaper are held on your device only, in the browser or app's own storage.
  • There is no analytics, no crash reporting, no advertising identifier, no tracking pixel and no third-party SDK of any kind.
  • The typeface, the artwork and the sync library are all part of the app rather than fetched from somewhere else, so opening it contacts nobody — not us and not a content delivery network.

With an account

An account exists so the same list appears on your phone and your laptop. It is optional and can be deleted from inside the app at any time.

WhatWhyKept
Email address It is how you sign in, and where a password reset goes. Until you delete the account
Password Stored only as a salted hash by our authentication provider. Nobody, including us, can read it. Until you delete the account
Your tasks, topics, history and settings So they are the same on every device you sign in on. Until you delete the account

Your data is held in one row, readable and writable only by you. That is enforced by the database itself, not by our code being careful: four row-level-security policies each say the same thing, which is that this row is yours or it does not exist as far as any other request is concerned.

Who else sees it

One sub-processor: Supabase, which hosts the database and handles sign-in. They process your data on our behalf and for no other purpose.

Nothing is sold. Nothing is shared with advertisers. There are no advertisers. Nothing is used to train anything.

Notifications, Face ID and the widget

  • The daily reminder is scheduled by your own phone and delivered by your own phone. There is no push server, no device token, and nothing is sent anywhere to make it arrive.
  • Face ID, Touch ID or a fingerprint, if you turn the lock on, is checked entirely by the operating system. The app is told yes or no and stores neither. It never receives or holds biometric data.
  • The home-screen widget reads a short line of text the app leaves for it in shared storage on the same device. It has no network access.

Deleting everything

In the app: your profile → Delete my account. It removes the account and everything stored with it immediately and permanently, and offers to wipe the copy on that device at the same time.

If you have already uninstalled the app, use the deletion page, which needs nothing but your email and password.

Deleted means deleted: the row and the account are removed, not flagged. Backups held by our host roll off on their own schedule and are never restored selectively.

Children

One Thing is not directed at children under 13 and collects nothing that would identify one. There is no profile, no social feature, no message and nobody else to see.

Your rights

You can see everything held about you inside the app — it is the app — and you can delete all of it yourself without asking. For anything else, including a copy of your data as a file, write to the address below and it will be dealt with within 30 days.

Changes

If this ever changes in a way that affects what is collected, the app will say so before the change takes effect rather than quietly updating a date at the top of a page.
